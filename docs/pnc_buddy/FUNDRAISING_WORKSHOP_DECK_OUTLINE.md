# Fundraising Capability Workshop | Deck Outline

Taleemabad | People and Culture
Version 1.0 | 6 September 2026

Ten slides. The deck is there to hold the session together, not to teach from. Most slides carry a question rather than content, and the facilitator notes are where the substance sits.

Roles rather than names are used throughout. Facilitator is the Senior Manager Fundraising.

---

## Slide 1. Why we are here

**On screen**
- Two and a half hours, mostly working through our own material
- Not a training session
- By the end: a shared picture of how fundraising moves, and where our work sits in it

**Visual:** title slide, plain.

**Facilitator notes:** Say what prompted this and be honest that it started as a P&C piece of work. Set the expectation that the session will surface things we cannot answer yet, and that this is the point rather than a problem. Mention that individual development plans come later and separately, so nobody is sitting through this wondering if they are being assessed.

**Discussion:** none. Keep this short.

---

## Slide 2. How fundraising moves

**On screen**
- Research → Qualify → Cultivate → Ask → Proposal → Budget → Due Diligence → Close → Deliver and Report → Steward → Renew

**Visual:** the eleven stages as a single horizontal flow across the slide, ideally on one line with small type rather than wrapped. No icons.

**Facilitator notes:** Walk it once, quickly. The one point worth making is that proposal writing is one stage, and most of what decides the outcome happens before it. Do not explain every stage, the group will ask about the ones they do not recognise.

**Discussion:** Where do we currently spend most of our time? Which stage do we do least well? Mark both on the slide as they answer.

---

## Slide 3. Can we answer these?

**On screen**
- Who should fund us? Why are we a fit? What are we asking them to fund?
- What problem are we solving? Does it work? How do we know?
- What does it cost? Can it scale? What would more funding enable?
- Why Taleemabad? How do we build and keep the relationship?

**Visual:** the eleven questions as a plain list, no numbering ornament. Nothing else on the slide.

**Facilitator notes:** Ask them cold, one at a time, and let the silences sit. Write down what we cannot answer on a flipchart. Separate two kinds of gap as they come up: things the person does not know, and things the organisation has never written down. The second kind is not their failure and should be recorded as an action for us.

**Discussion:** the whole slide is the discussion. Allow 10 minutes at least.

---

## Slide 4. How well do we know Taleemabad?

**On screen**
- What problem do we solve, and for whom?
- Where do we work, and how many people do we reach?
- What is our evidence, and how strong is it?
- What does it cost, and who funds us today?
- What is different about us, and what are we scaling?

**Visual:** rapid-fire question list. Deliberately no answers on the slide.

**Facilitator notes:** Run this fast, no notes allowed. Then slow down for the one that matters: ask each person for 60 seconds on what Taleemabad is, and then separately 60 seconds on why someone should fund us. These come out as the same answer the first time, and separating them is the useful part.

Do not put our reach, cost or evidence figures on this slide until they have been confirmed. **[To be added/validated with Programmes and Finance.]**

**Discussion:** Which of these could you answer confidently in front of a donor tomorrow?

## Slide 5. Finding a donor is not the same as having an opportunity

**On screen**
- Research tells us who they are. Qualification tells us whether to spend our time.
- Nine things to weigh: mission, geography, programme, evidence, scale, ticket size, relationship, timing, effort
- The call: pursue, cultivate, monitor or decline
- Record why we said no

**Visual:** the nine qualification dimensions as a simple scoring row, 1 to 5 against each.

**Facilitator notes:** Give each person one funder from Mulago, Vitol, GiveWell or DIV, and 20 minutes. The question they answer is whether we should pursue, and why.

Watch for the Vitol case. Vitol does not take unsolicited applications, they invite organisations through a programme manager. If someone recommends writing a proposal, that is a good moment to draw out the difference between research and qualification.

When reviewing, focus on the quality of the recommendation rather than how much was found.

**Discussion:** What would change your mind about this funder?

## Slide 6. Building the funding case

**On screen**
- Problem → Intervention → Evidence → Economics → Ask → Scale
- The story on its own does not survive a donor conversation
- The numbers on its own do not give anyone a reason to care

**Visual:** six blocks left to right, equal weight, no arrows implying difficulty.

**Facilitator notes:** Use one real Taleemabad intervention **[To be added/validated with Fundraising and Programmes]**. Ask for a one page case covering the six blocks, plus a separate list of what information is missing and who owns getting it.

The missing information list is the output worth pressing on. Saying "Finance needs to confirm this" is the right instinct.

**Discussion:** What is the weakest block for this intervention right now?

---

## Slide 7. Understanding the numbers

**On screen**
- Programme cost, direct and indirect
- Cost per child, per school, per teacher
- Cost per outcome, where we have the data
- Restricted and unrestricted, co-funding, funding gap
- We are not replacing Finance. We need to explain the case and know when to bring them in.

**Visual:** one worked cost breakdown, real figures once cleared. Leave it blank rather than filling it with example numbers. **[To be added/validated with Finance.]**

**Facilitator notes:** Have them calculate cost per child from the real budget, then ask what else they would need in order to calculate cost per outcome. Then the donor questions: why does it cost this much, what would another PKR 10M enable, what happens if we get half, what changes at scale.

If the real budget is not cleared in time, cut this section short rather than running it on invented numbers.

**Discussion:** Which numbers would you want Finance to confirm before you sat in front of a donor?

---

## Slide 8. Let us meet the donor

**On screen**
- Role-play, 25 minutes
- No pitching for the first five minutes
- Your job at the start is to understand them

**Visual:** just the rule. One line, large.

**Facilitator notes:** Play the donor. Hold the five minute rule firmly, people will try to pitch. Then move to the harder questions:

- Why Taleemabad?
- How do you know this works?
- What does it cost?
- What would my funding enable?
- What happens when my grant ends?
- How could this scale, and who pays for it?

Afterwards, ask each person what they would pitch differently based on what they heard, not on what they prepared.

**Discussion:** Which question was hardest, and what would we need in order to answer it properly next time?

---

## Slide 9. What we are building

**On screen**
- Understanding our organisation · Donor research · Donor qualification · Stakeholder mapping
- Donor engagement and listening · Storytelling and pitching · Proposal development
- Financial literacy · Evidence and M&E literacy · Cost-effectiveness and scale
- Pipeline management · Stewardship and ethics

**Visual:** the twelve as a plain grid. No scores, no names, no allocation of who owns what.

**Facilitator notes:** This is a proposed list, not a finalised competency framework, and it is worth saying so. Do not present any split of responsibilities between the two team members here. That is a conversation for the Senior Manager Fundraising with each of them afterwards, and putting it on a slide would make it look decided.

**Discussion:** Which four or five of these matter most for us over the next six months? Which are we already fine on?

---

## Slide 10. From workshop to actual work

**On screen**
- Workshop → real assignments → feedback → individual development plans → review at 30 to 60 days
- The assignments are where the development happens
- Each person's plan is agreed separately

**Visual:** simple left to right sequence, five steps.

**Facilitator notes:** Close by naming two or three concrete assignments coming out of the session, with owners and dates, so it does not end on an intention. Examples: scoring our current prospects against the qualification dimensions, building a compliance matrix from a live RFP, or agreeing with Finance which cost figures we are allowed to quote.

Say plainly that at 30 to 60 days the question will be what someone can now do that they could not before, and what piece of work shows it.

**Discussion:** What is each person taking away as their first assignment?
