### 18 August 2026 · "Quick eval sync" · 29 min
**Source:** Fathom — `https://fathom.video/share/kDbmL5aH6jr8wCcjt2sXFFxqKAfVFH2-`
⚠️ **Transcript supplied to me cuts off at 16:19 of 29 min — the last ~13 minutes are not covered below.**

#### What happened

**Unsa — auditing the Digital Coach (DC) standards against what audio can actually detect.**
Unsa brought the core problem: most of the pedagogical standards were written assuming the four services (lesson plan, digital coach, training, assessment) are linked, and assuming a classroom observation. Isolate DC from the lesson plan and very few standards remain touchable. Her worked examples:
- *"Does the teacher provide differential support during UDO?"* — the teacher may be doing it by moving between children, and audio cannot capture that.
- A standard about a student *"thinking, trying and not giving up"* is written as **students**, plural — so is it the whole classroom, and does the whole class have to be on the same page?
- A standard requiring the DC to identify error patterns and report that *"X% of the class is confused about Y concept"* presumes a report against a specific lesson plan.

**Ahwaz's direction on it.** He treated the finding as the point of the exercise: *"that's exactly what we wanted to uncover"*, and accepted that **the standards will have to evolve** — but insisted we provide a justification so the change doesn't read as sudden. He asked Unsa to mark every standard row into one of three buckets:
1. **Achievable, worth aiming for** — fixable with better recording, better data analysis, pipeline work (speech-to-text, diarization).
2. **Not achievable with pure DC, but achievable with a human in the loop** — with his own caveat that this starts to collapse into traditional coaching where the audio just helps fill out the forms.
3. **Not a practical standard at all** — and we should be straightforward about saying so.

He asked for a **recommendation per standard**, not just a classification. Unsa scoped it to purely audio recordings, and committed to having it ready that night or the next morning. Training and assessment not started — DC first, then the others.

**Momna — the lesson-plan standards.**
- Some standards are **fully LP-dependent and still not assessable**, because the actions they describe aren't in the LP script — e.g. *"teacher circulates and provides differential support"* needs classroom data, so she failed it on review.
- Some entries are **undocumented and unintelligible** — she named **P5A**, written as *"LP App, SLO Picker, Coverage Map, Block Submit Without"*: she has read the description and cannot tell what it means. She needs proper descriptions before she can improve them.
- Her proposal: **simplify the indicator explanations and add explicit pass/fail criteria**.
- Ahwaz asked whether she is using the **checker tool Shujan built** into the standard. Momna has Shujan's document but says the AI reviewer carries more standards than it.
- Unsa added that there is a **new region-wise AI reviewer document** with region-specific demands, sitting on Kenya's page.

**The principle that emerged (Ahwaz):** *"if a standard is not applied consistently across regions, then it's not really a standard."* Momna agreed to go to **Mariam and Rida** about Kenya and collect the AI reviewer files each region actually uses, to see what is genuinely in play.

**Ahwaz pushed on the output.** What is the deliverable? A **core set of standards that holds across regions**, plus a defined band of legitimate region-by-region variation — with the product's core pedagogy (gradual release: *I do / we do / you do*) as the non-negotiable spine. Momna's framing: the standards as they stand are not practical or reliable, so we should define the set we can actually apply across regions and then measure how much a single standard can legitimately differ region to region.

#### Where this team is headed (rolling read)

The through-line across both days is **shrinking the standards framework down to what is actually measurable**, and being honest in public about what isn't:

1. **Per service, per channel:** classify every standard by what can genuinely detect it — DC audio alone (22), human coach required (7), lesson-plan-dependent, or not practical at all. DC is done; **training and assessment are still to do**.
2. **Per region:** a standard that isn't applied consistently across regions isn't a standard. Core cross-region set + a defined band of legitimate regional variation, with the product's gradual-release spine as non-negotiable.
3. **Evidence discipline:** every standard carries its research citation, and the citations get verified rather than trusted.
4. **Usability:** simplify the indicator explanations, add explicit pass/fail criteria, and document the entries nobody can currently interpret (P5A).
5. **Then measure:** baseline error analysis on real lesson plans, region by region, and report the gap back to the regions.
