# Fundraising Capability Workshop | Facilitator Guide

Taleemabad | People and Culture
Version 1.0 | 6 September 2026

This is the short version, for use while running the session. The research and the fuller reasoning sit in the working guide, `Fundraising Capability | P&C + Fundraising Working Guide`.

Roles rather than names are used throughout. The facilitator is the Senior Manager Fundraising. The two participants are the fundraising team members.

---

## 1. Why we are doing this

We have two people in the fundraising team who are capable and who are currently working mostly on the parts of fundraising that are visible: research when it is asked for, and proposals when there is a deadline. What we would like to build is a shared understanding of the whole process, so that both of them can see where a piece of work sits and what has to be true before it moves forward.

This session is not training in the formal sense. It is a working session using our own material, and most of the value should come from the exercises rather than from anything presented.

**By the end of the session, we want the team to have:**

- a clearer picture of how a funding relationship moves from research through to renewal, and where their own work fits
- more confidence answering the basic questions a donor asks about Taleemabad, our costs and our evidence
- a first go at qualifying a funder and building a funding case, with feedback
- a sense of where each of them is strong and where they want support

**Duration:** roughly two and a half hours. It can be split into two sittings if that works better, with sections 1 to 3 in the first and 4 to 7 in the second.

## 2. How we think about fundraising

Use this to open. The point is a shared vocabulary, not a process to comply with.

**Research → Qualify → Cultivate → Design the Ask → Proposal → Budget → Due Diligence → Close → Deliver and Report → Steward → Renew**

| Stage | In short |
|---|---|
| Research | Finding out who funds work like ours, and how they actually fund it |
| Qualify | Deciding whether this one is worth our time |
| Cultivate | Building understanding and trust before we ask |
| Design the ask | Deciding what specifically we are asking them to fund, and for how much |
| Proposal | Answering what they asked, in their format |
| Budget | The numbers behind the ask, and the assumptions under them |
| Due diligence | Their checks on us: governance, finance, safeguarding, evidence |
| Close | Agreement, contracting, conditions |
| Deliver and report | Doing the work and reporting on it honestly |
| Steward | Staying in contact, including when we are not asking for anything |
| Renew | The next conversation, which is easier if the previous stages were done well |

For each stage, four questions worth putting to the group:

- What are we trying to achieve here?
- What should come out of this stage?
- Who should own it?
- What would good work look like?

We have deliberately left these open. Working through even three or four stages together in the room is more useful than presenting answers, and the answers will differ by funder type.

## 3. Questions we want every fundraiser to be able to answer

Put these up and ask them cold, one at a time.

1. Who should fund us?
2. Why are we a fit?
3. What exactly are we asking them to fund?
4. What problem are we solving?
5. Does our solution work?
6. How do we know?
7. What does it cost?
8. Can it scale?
9. What would additional funding enable?
10. Why Taleemabad?
11. How do we build and maintain that relationship?

Write down anything the group cannot answer. Some of those gaps will be individual knowledge, and some will be things the organisation has not documented anywhere. Worth separating the two, because they need different fixes.

## 4. Capabilities we want to build

| Capability | What we would expect to see in practice |
|---|---|
| Understanding our own organisation | Explains Taleemabad and why someone should fund it, without notes, and knows which claims we can defend |
| Donor research | Can say what a funder funds, where, at what size, through what route, and who the relevant people are |
| Donor qualification | Reaches a clear call on whether to pursue, and can defend a no as well as a yes |
| Stakeholder mapping | Knows who decides, who influences, and how we would get in front of them |
| Donor engagement and listening | Comes out of a first conversation able to describe what the funder cares about in their words |
| Storytelling and pitching | Puts the problem, the intervention, the evidence and the economics into one case |
| Proposal and grant development | Works from the funder's requirements, flags what is missing rather than filling gaps |
| Financial literacy | Can explain what a programme costs and what drives it, and knows when to bring Finance in |
| Evidence and M&E literacy | Understands what our data shows, and is clear about what it does not |
| Cost-effectiveness and scale | Can discuss value for money against alternatives, and a realistic route to scale |
| Pipeline management | Keeps owner, next action, value and status current |
| Donor stewardship and ethics | Reports accurately, keeps the relationship going, does not overstate to win funding |

Twelve is more than we can work on at once. Part of the closing discussion is which four or five matter most over the next six months.

## 5. Practice

Most of the session. Use real Taleemabad material wherever it is available.

| What we are testing | Task | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge of the organisation | 60 second explanation of Taleemabad, then separately, 60 seconds on why someone should fund us | Two distinct answers, plus a list of what nobody could answer |
| Donor research and judgement | One funder each from Mulago, Vitol, GiveWell or DIV. 20 minutes. Should we pursue them, and why | A scored qualification sheet and a recommendation with reasons |
| Building a funding case | Take one real Taleemabad intervention **[To be added/validated with Fundraising / Programmes]** and answer: who funds it, why are we a fit, what exactly are we asking for, what evidence do we have, what is missing, what does it cost, what would more funding enable, can it scale, who pays at scale | A one page case, plus a list of information gaps with owners |
| Reading a budget | Using a real programme budget **[To be added/validated with Finance]**: calculate cost per child, say what else would be needed for cost per outcome, and answer what another PKR 10M would enable | One calculated unit cost and a list of what Finance needs to confirm |
| Donor conversation | Role-play. Facilitator plays the donor. No pitching for the first five minutes, the participant's job is to understand the donor | One thing each person would change in their pitch based on what they heard |

On the funder exercise, one thing worth watching for: Vitol does not accept unsolicited applications and invites organisations through a programme manager. If someone recommends submitting a proposal, that is a useful correction to make in the room, because it shows the difference between a research answer and a qualification answer.

When reviewing the research, focus on the quality of the recommendation rather than the volume of information collected.

## 6. Finance and evidence

Fundraisers are not expected to replace Finance. They should understand the numbers well enough to explain the funding case and to recognise when Finance needs to step in.

What the team should be comfortable with:

- programme cost, and what drives it
- direct and indirect costs
- cost per child, and cost per school or teacher where that is the more useful unit
- cost per outcome, where we have enough data to calculate it
- budget assumptions
- restricted and unrestricted funding
- co-funding
- funding gap

Questions to put to them, in the voice of a donor:

- Why does this programme cost this much?
- What would another PKR 10M enable?
- What happens if we receive only half of the ask?
- What changes financially if we scale?
- Which numbers do we need Finance to validate before we talk to a donor?

"I will check that with Finance and come back to you today" is a good answer in a donor meeting. A number that turns out to be wrong is not recoverable in the same way.

On evidence, the same principle. The team should know which of our results come from external evaluation and which are internal monitoring, and should be comfortable saying we do not have that evidence yet.

## 7. What happens afterward

The workshop is a starting point, not the development plan.

- Each team member will have their own development plan, agreed separately.
- The Senior Manager Fundraising validates what each of them should prioritise, since P&C's view of this is based on the roles rather than on how the work actually runs.
- Most of the development should come through real assignments with feedback, rather than further sessions.
- Worth reviewing progress after 30 to 60 days, looking at what someone can now do that they could not before, and what piece of work shows it.
