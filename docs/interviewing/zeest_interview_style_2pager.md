
# How Zeest Interviews

## The Themes She Asks Around

**Evidence:** 3 Growth Manager case-study debriefs — Salman Tariq (10 Aug) · Muhammad Waqas (11 Aug) · Ahmad Wajahat Sheikh (12 Aug)

> **The one thing every question serves**
>
> Can this person **get into a room they have no right to be in, and change the mind of the person sitting in it** — without lying, without paying, and without needing a relationship they already had. Her words, in all three calls: *“building relationships and sustaining them and manipulating conversations for your organization’s incentive is the heart of the role.”*
>

| Theme | What she is testing | Asked |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Charm the room** | Persuasion over diagnosis. Research is table stakes; she wants the move that changes a mind in the meeting. One candidate was told his answers were too diagnosis-heavy. | **3/3** |
| **2. Access, and the end-run** | Not “do you have contacts” but: who would you call — and if you know nobody, how do you manufacture a way in? She rewards a named mechanism, not willingness. | **3/3** |
| **3. Translating our incentive into their mandate** | Selling the same product in the counterpart’s vocabulary. Her example: they want teacher training, not AI — so sell it as teacher training. | **3/3** |
| **4. Are the relationships portable?** | Whether the skill travels or is an artefact of one long tenure. Narrows to individuals, and to cold opens: would **all** of them pick up your call? | **3/3** |
| **5. Persistence under escalation** | She closes each escape route until only a decision remains — no budget, no time, no explanation, 50 jobs at risk — then: *“really, what would you do?”* | **3/3** |
| **6. The ethics floor** | A kickback demand, with hospitality separated from corruption so they cannot hide in the ambiguity. Asked before the company position is disclosed. | **3/3** |
| **7. Ownership, not narration** | Did they carry it or watch it? One deal inside a headline CV number; unfunded→funded; and defining their own jargon — which caught AI-written content once. | 2/3 |
| **8. Skill transfer across sectors** | For candidates crossing from NGO or private into government. Non-work evidence explicitly counts — bureaucracy navigated in your own life. | 2/3 |
| **9. Appetite for the job as it is** | Government-only, slow, rejection-heavy — asked of the candidate whose ambition pointed elsewhere. | 1/3 |

### How she runs the hour

- **Discloses her method first**, and warns a thought experiment is coming — no ambush, no loss of difficulty.
- **Reuses the same scenarios** on every candidate (PECTA blocked pilot, kickback, “a day in this role”). This is what makes candidates comparable — the most copyable part of her method.
- **Supplies real context generously** — Rumi’s mechanics, the Prevail-funded Rawalpindi pilot, the PECTA collapse. A thinking test, not a research test.
- **Plays the counterpart herself**, irritated rather than neutral: “this is the entirety of the government we deal with. **This is the baseline.**”
- **Protects the candidate’s dignity** — “it won’t count against you” on a power cut; “I’m not married to English”; teaches rather than traps.

# The Questions She Asks

## Verbatim and generalised, grouped by purpose

### Opening — role comprehension  [3/3, always her first question]

- “What do you think a day in this role looks like?” — asked before any praise or correction, then let the silence run.

### Interrogating the case study

- Name one strength, then stress it: “you made sure the success indicators were very pointed — now how would you **secure** that indicator?”
- “Why did you choose **this** arm of government, as opposed to [two named alternatives]?”
- “You wrote [term]. What do **you** understand when you say [term]?” — the cheapest AI-detection tool there is.
- “You leaned into [company value] in your one-pager. How have you done that in your own work — and any example you can come up with?”

### Verifying the CV

- “Walk me through **one deal inside that number** — what did they pay for, how did you convince them, what almost killed it?” Unbluffable.

### Access and end-runs  [3/3, her most-pressed line]

- “Who would you call? And if you know nobody — how do you get in the room?”
- “Assume this is the **first time** we are entering the province, with no existing relationship. How do you push yourself into the room?”
- “How do you turn somebody you merely know into somebody who gives you access?”
- Live cold call: “I have the secretary’s number and no relationship. **Call him.** How does that go?”

### Relationship durability — the portability trap

- “Could you go back to any organisation from the last five or six years, pick up a contact, and convert that capital today?”
- “Would **all** of them pick up your call — or only some? Which ones, and why?”
- “Give me a **cold open** with someone influential, whom you sustained, and could comfortably call today.”

### Ownership and skill transfer

- “Have you taken something from **unfunded to funded** — or were you supporting a team that did?” / “moved a free pilot to a paid deal?”
- “Your experience is donor/NGO/private. How does that translate to government — **and it needn’t be a work example**; bureaucracy in your own life counts.”
- If the honest answer is no, widen the credit to any leverage they created — and note whether they invented an example instead.

### Escalating scenarios — always real Taleemabad situations

- **Blocked pilot (PECTA):** approval authority withdrawn mid-deal, the CEO ghosting, the minister unreachable. What now?
- **Renewal squeeze:** one month of payroll left, 50 jobs at risk, no-cost extension dead, and they won’t say what changed. “Really, what would you do?”
- **Three-way partnership:** they want it, they have no money. Bring a funder in without losing their ownership of it.
- **Category refusal:** “we don’t want AI, we want teacher training — end of story.” Sell the same product in their words.
- **The convening:** 60 days, no budget line, fill the room with secretaries and partners. Who first — and why not the top?

### Running the role-play the way she does

- Give the product and deal history in full first — if they don’t know the product, **stop, teach them, restart**. Offer one to three minutes’ prep.
- Name the counterpart and their real constraint; constrain the scene — ten minutes, mid-meeting, others in the room.
- Play them **irritated, not neutral**, escalating objections in order: accountability, then budget cannibalisation, then scale.
- Correct third-person narration at once: “talk to me, not about them.” Close explicitly and thank them.

### Ethics gate — ask, probe, then disclose

- “You have the green light, but they want 1–3% of the contract in someone’s pocket. How do you manage that conversation?”
- Remove the ambiguity yourself — “I mean 3% of the entire project cost, high-level bribes”, not chai-pani — then probe once: “so if your organisation green-lit it, you’d go ahead?”
- **Only then** state the position: hospitality is fine; skimming is not, and we walk away from the money rather than pay.

### Close

- “Is there anything I should know before I make this decision?” · Fit, not capability: “how do you feel that we don’t work with the private sector at all?” · Then take their questions, candidly.
