## How to read this document

This is not a model essay and candidates should not be expected to match its wording. It is the
**Strong Yes line** — what an excellent RM actually produces under time pressure, assumptions
labelled, some rough edges intact. It is not the ceiling.

Three rules carry over from the growth-case rubric and apply here without change:

- **Score reasoning, not agreement.** A candidate who reaches a different conclusion and defends it
  with the numbers scores as high as one who matches this answer. Several questions here have more
  than one defensible answer, and where that is true it is stated.
- **Every score cites evidence.** Quote the line, page or slide. A dimension score with nothing
  behind it is an impression, not a score.
- **Distinguish wrong from different.** *Wrong* = contradicted by the case data. *Definitional* = a
  different but defensible reading, stated as such — do not penalise. *Fabricated* = a figure or
  fact that does not exist in the case — treat as serious.

**Format is not a scoring dimension.** The brief explicitly permits "a written document or slides",
so a deck is not a lesser submission than a document, and an executive summary was **required**, not
a bonus.

### The one forced reading in the case

"Students already below grade level: 34% / Students at grade level: 75%" is **ambiguously worded**.
It cannot be a composition share: 34+75 = **109%** for Urban and 49+61 = **110%** for Rural, both
impossible. The only internally consistent reading is **pass rates within each subgroup**, and on
that reading both regions' overall rates reconcile to the stated figures exactly. A candidate who
states this interpretation explicitly is showing real data discipline. A candidate who reads it as
composition and proceeds without noticing the impossibility has made a **factual error**.

### The signal the whole case is built on

The adoption gap is enormous and it **does not propagate**:

> 37pp LP adoption gap → 41pp DC gap → **0.3 FICO gap** → 6pp dipstick gap → 11pp pass-rate gap,
> and that last 11pp is largely **composition**, not teaching.

Urban has roughly twice the adoption and almost none of the practice advantage it implies. That
break in the chain is the finding. Everything else in the case tests whether the candidate acts on
it or is captured by the dashboard.

## What the case is actually testing (the traps)

1. **Dashboard literalism.** Ranking regions off the six headline indicators. The Programme
   Manager's "I don't want explanations, tell me which region is better" is bait — the honest answer
   is that the question as posed cannot be answered from this data, delivered *without* sounding
   evasive.
2. **Directional bias, not noise.** The five data faults are not random. Offline/unsynced DC
   observations, failed syncs and manual record-keeping all cluster in the **connectivity-poor Rural
   region**, so Rural's numbers are **understated**. Urban's LP adoption is **inflated in meaning** —
   coaches say teachers complete LPs *because adoption is tracked*. The true gap is narrower than the
   dashboard shows at **both** ends. Weak answers say "the data is unreliable" and stop; strong
   answers state the **direction** of the error.
3. **Compliance vs implementation.** Urban's 76% LP adoption with flat FICO is Goodhart's law in the
   field: a tracked metric became a target and stopped measuring practice.
4. **Composition vs value-added.** See the ground-truth table. Leadership's "Urban is clearly
   performing better" is the trap; Rural does **better with the students who are hardest to move**.
5. **Arithmetic honesty on capacity.** The case says reduce by 15%. It also demands +25%
   observations, which the candidate must **price** (+6.25pp). Answers that cut exactly 15pp and
   ignore the new demand have not done the arithmetic.
6. **The donor visit as an integrity test.** "We cannot afford to show them a school where things
   aren't working" versus the donor's own stated wish to see **challenging contexts**. Staging the
   easy school is the failure mode. The donor's brief actually licenses honesty.
7. **The two constraints with one solution.** Research says extra coaching contaminates the
   experiment; coaches say they have no capacity. **Both are solved by the same move** — keep coaches
   out of the experiment. Spotting that the constraints are complementary rather than competing is
   the single best discriminator in the case.
8. **Defensiveness with the AEO.** The rain and the device fault are true and are *not* an answer.
   The AEO has also independently corroborated the data-integrity problem, which makes them a
   witness, not just a complainant.

## Part A — Strategic Diagnosis

### Question 1 — The 10 pieces of information (10 points)

The question demands four things per item: **what, why, where from, and which decision it changes**.
An item missing the "which decision" clause is incomplete — that clause is what separates an
information *list* from an information *strategy*.

A strong ten covers these territories (wording will vary):

| # | Information | Why | Source | Decision it changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sync/error logs: which records failed to upload, by school and date | Establishes the **direction and size** of the data error before any comparison | Platform/MIS logs, coach manual registers | Whether any region comparison is publishable at all |
| 2 | LP *implementation* quality vs LP *completion* | Urban completion is compliance-driven | Classroom observation notes, work samples | Whether Urban's 76% means anything |
| 3 | Coach-visit dosage per school (actual visits, last 8 weeks) | Rural's own evidence says consistency drives practice | Coach logs cross-checked with school registers | Where to concentrate scarce coach time |
| 4 | Student assessment composition (baseline grade level by school) | Overall pass rates are not comparable across different mixes | Assessment data | Whether "Urban is better" survives |
| 5 | Baseline→endline growth per student, not point-in-time pass rate | Value-added is the only fair cross-region measure | Assessment system | The entire performance narrative |
| 6 | FICO sub-component scores | A flat composite hides which practices moved | Observation instrument | Which specific practice the 90 days targets |
| 7 | Connectivity/device status by school | Separates *won't use* from *can't use* | Field audit, device logs | Whether Rural needs an offline model or a demand fix |
| 8 | Actual coach time-on-task by activity | The 115% is an estimate; the cut must be real | Time logs / one-week sample | Which activity to cut in Q4 |
| 9 | School closure and access calendar (incl. rain-affected routes) | Distinguishes non-delivery from non-recording | AEO, school heads | The AEO action plan |
| 10 | Teacher workload/burden perception | Urban teachers privately report overwhelm | Short pulse survey, coach conversations | Whether to add or remove activities |

**Anchors.** **5** — ten genuinely decision-linked items spanning data integrity, practice, outcomes,
capacity and access; at least one aimed at *its own* assumptions. **3** — ten sensible items with
sources named, but several are "more data" with no decision attached. **1** — a generic list of
reports, fewer than ten, or items that merely restate the dashboard.

### Question 2 — Can you conclude Urban is performing better? (15 points)

**The benchmark answer: No — and the reason is not "the data is bad".** The defensible position is
that the comparison as posed is **invalid**, and the case gives enough to say *why* along each of the
six axes the question names.

- **Data reliability.** Faults are **directionally biased toward Rural** (offline DC observations,
  failed syncs, manual registers, closed-school visits). Rural is understated. Any Urban lead is
  overstated by an unknown but non-zero margin. *State the direction, not just the existence.*
- **Adoption.** Urban genuinely leads (76 vs 39; 72 vs 31). This much is real and should be conceded.
- **Implementation quality.** Urban's own coaches report LPs completed **for tracking, not use**.
  Adoption is therefore not evidence of practice in Urban.
- **Teacher practice.** FICO 2.9 vs 2.6 — a **0.3 gap on a 37pp adoption gap**. The chain breaks
  here, and this is the single most important sentence in the answer.
- **Student outcomes.** Urban 67% vs Rural 56% overall, but Rural carries **≈2.1× the share of
  below-grade-level students** and passes **49% of them vs Urban's 34%**. Urban leads on at-grade
  students (75 vs 61). The regions are serving different populations; the headline is composition.
- **Infrastructure.** Rural's constraint is capability (connectivity, devices, access); Urban's is
  motivation/burden. **These require opposite interventions**, which is why the ranking question is
  not merely unanswerable but the wrong question.

**The Programme Manager still needs an answer.** A top submission does not hide behind nuance. It
gives a usable verdict: *"On adoption, Urban leads and that is real. On teaching practice and on
moving the hardest students, Rural is at least as strong and probably stronger. I can give you a
defensible ranking in two weeks once sync is fixed and I have growth rather than pass rates. What I
would not do is expand on the current numbers."* Answering the question **and** correcting its
premise is the mark of a 5.

**Anchors.** **5** — reaches "not comparable", proves it with the composition arithmetic **and** the
broken adoption→FICO chain, names the direction of the data bias, and still hands leadership a
decision. **3** — notes data problems and the compliance issue, treats the student data as a simple
Urban win or hedges without resolving it, no composition insight. **1** — ranks Urban better off the
dashboard, or refuses to answer while citing "unreliable data".

### Question 3 — Three root causes of low Rural adoption + how to test (10 points)

Each cause must come with a **falsification test that runs before** any intervention. A cause with a
plan attached instead of a test scores 3 at best.

| Root cause | Test before intervening |
|---|---|
| **Infrastructure — can't, not won't** (connectivity, devices, sync) | Pull sync logs and device status per school; compare *attempted* vs *completed* actions. High attempts with low completions means infrastructure, not demand. Cheap and fast. |
| **Access/dosage** — coaches physically not reaching schools (rain routes, closures) | Reconcile coach logs against school registers/AEO records for the last 8 weeks. If adoption tracks visit frequency, dosage is the cause. |
| **Measurement artefact** — Rural adoption is happening but not recording | Take a stratified sample of ~10 Rural schools, verify use directly, compare against what the dashboard shows for those same schools. The delta *is* the size of the reporting error. |

Other defensible candidates: teacher confidence/training depth, LP relevance to multi-grade rural
classrooms, headteacher support. Any is fine **if paired with a real test**.

**Excellence marker:** ordering the tests by cost and speed, and noting that the third test
**quantifies the error in every other number in the case** — so it should run first.

**Anchors.** **5** — three distinct plausible causes, each with a specific cheap falsification test,
sampling logic sound. **3** — sensible causes, vague tests ("we would investigate"). **1** — causes
asserted as fact, or interventions proposed with no test at all.

## Part B — Forced Prioritization

### Question 4 — Continue → Reduce → Pause → Delegate → Redesign (15 points)

**The arithmetic must close.** The table sums to 115%. The stated requirement is a 15pp cut. The
+25% observation demand adds **≈6.25pp**, so a candidate who accepts that demand must actually find
**≈21pp**. Three responses are defensible: fund it, refuse it with evidence, or trade it. Silently
ignoring it is the common failure.

A defensible worked cut:

| Activity | Now | Action | After | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HITL observations | 25% | **Continue / protect** | 25% | Very High, and the only activity with direct evidence of changing practice |
| Teacher training | 20% | **Redesign** (cluster/peer-led) | 15% | Very High, but delivery mode is negotiable |
| LP implementation | 15% | **Continue** | 15% | Very High; the core of the programme |
| DC observations | 20% | **Reduce** to verified sampling, not census | 10% | High, but heavily affected by the sync fault — generating unreliable data at full cost |
| LE experiments | 15% | **Redesign + delegate to LE team** | 5% | Solves the research contamination problem at the same time (see Q7) |
| Student assessments | 10% | **Reduce** to a sampled cycle | 7% | High; needed for outcomes but not at census frequency in a 90-day window |
| Reporting / data validation | 10% | **Redesign, do not cut** | 8% | 🔴 The instinct is to cut this. Cutting it deepens the problem the whole case is about. Fix sync at source so manual duplication ends and the *same* work costs less. |
| **Total** | **115%** | | **85%** | Leaves ~15pp headroom for the observation increase and the two absent coaches |

**The discriminator: reporting/data validation.** It looks like pure overhead and is the cheapest
thing to cut. Cutting it is **wrong** — every other decision in the case depends on data that is
already failing, and coaches are duplicating records manually *because* the system is broken. Fixing
the cause removes the cost. Candidates who cut it to hit their number have optimised the arithmetic
and damaged the programme; say so.

**Anchors.** **5** — arithmetic closes and is shown; every line justified against strategic
importance; prices the +25% demand; protects data validation with a stated reason; uses all five
verbs meaningfully rather than relabelling cuts. **3** — reaches ≥15pp with reasonable choices but
does not price the observation increase, or cuts reporting without noticing the consequence. **1** —
no arithmetic, percentages that do not sum, or "work more efficiently" as the mechanism.

### Question 5 — Responding to "everything is a priority" (10 points)

The test is **professional pushback**: making the trade-off visible without sounding resistant. The
move is to accept the goal and surrender the *choice*, not the *work*.

1. **Agree with the objective.** Every item is genuinely important; do not argue it.
2. **Make the constraint arithmetic, not emotional.** 115% (121% with the new demand) against 100%
   available. Numbers depersonalise the conversation.
3. **Present a costed menu, not a refusal.** "Here is full delivery of all seven; here is what I can
   guarantee at 100%; here are three options for the difference."
4. **Name what breaks and when.** Quality degrades silently before it fails visibly, and attrition
   risk is live — two coaches are already considering resignation.
5. **Hand the decision back with a recommendation.** "I recommend option B. If you prefer A, I need
   X. Either way I will tell you what I am pausing so nothing disappears without you knowing."
6. **Ask for a standing rule.** One initiative in, one out, for the next 90 days.

**Excellence markers:** offering to be held to the outcome rather than the activity count;
volunteering a date to revisit; framing it as protecting *leadership's* credibility at the coming
review, since a programme that quietly under-delivers in front of a donor is the real risk.

**Anchors.** **5** — costed, unemotional, gives leadership a real choice plus a recommendation, no
victimhood. **3** — explains the overload and asks for prioritisation but offers no menu or
recommendation. **1** — capitulates ("we will manage") or refuses ("this is not possible"); both fail.

## Part C — Coach Leadership

### Question 6 — Four individual management approaches (10 points)

Five required elements each: **discuss · support · expectation · measure · escalate**. Missing the
escalation clause is the most common gap and it matters — it is the difference between managing and
hoping. Approaches must be **genuinely different**; a template repeated four times scores 2.

**Coach A — high output, poor collaboration.** Name the value of the results first and mean it, then
be specific about the behaviour and its cost to others. Support: a defined role that uses the
strength (peer modelling, mentoring the two new coaches) so collaboration becomes a contribution
rather than a correction. Expectation: results maintained **and** two named collaborative behaviours.
Measure: peer feedback, participation, mentee progress. Escalate: unchanged after ~6–8 weeks, or any
conduct issue, moves to a formal conversation. *Trap: tolerating it because the numbers are good. In
an 18-coach team at 115%, one non-collaborator is a capacity tax on everyone else.*

**Coach B — great with teachers, misses reporting.** Diagnose before disciplining: capability,
tooling, or priority? Given the sync faults, some of this may be **the system, not the person** —
this is the coach most likely to be failing because of a broken tool. Support: fix the tooling,
simplify the format, offer a fixed weekly slot. Expectation: an agreed, minimal, non-negotiable
reporting standard with a stated deadline. Measure: on-time submission rate over 4 weeks. Escalate:
if it persists once tooling is fixed, it is a performance issue.

**Coach C — low output, strong potential and attitude.** A development case with a real timeline, not
indefinite patience. Discuss what specifically is hard. Support: pair with Coach A, model → observe →
feedback, narrow the school load to build wins. Expectation: a stepped target, not the full standard
immediately. Measure: fortnightly against the steps. Escalate: no movement across ~2–3 checkpoints
means revisiting role fit honestly.

**Coach D — strong but burning out.** 🔴 **Act first and fastest here.** This is the live attrition
risk in a team that cannot hire for three months, and it is partly **caused by the 115%**. Discuss
workload and wellbeing without making it a performance conversation. Support: reduce load *now* (a
direct call on the Q4 cut), redistribute schools, protect leave. Expectation: sustainable delivery,
explicitly lowered in the short term. Measure: workload hours, engagement, a check-in cadence.
Escalate: to People & Culture early if wellbeing is affected.

**Excellence marker:** connecting D (and the two resignation-considering coaches) back to the Q4
capacity decision, and treating B's reporting failure as possibly systemic. Both show the candidate
reading the case as one system rather than four isolated vignettes.

**Anchors.** **5** — four genuinely differentiated approaches, all five elements each, escalation
thresholds time-bound, at least one link back to the capacity problem. **3** — differentiated and
humane but escalation vague or one element consistently missing. **1** — one approach applied four
times, or performance-manages the burnout case.

## Part D — Experiment Management

### Question 7 — Approve / Delay / Reduce scope / Redesign / One region (10 points)

The question says **choose one**. Candidates who refuse to choose have failed the question's central
test even if their reasoning is good — say so, and cap accordingly.

**The strongest answer is D (Redesign)**, typically absorbing E (one region) and C (reduced scope)
inside the redesign. The reasoning that earns a 5:

> The research constraint and the capacity constraint have **the same solution**. Research warns that
> added coaching support contaminates results. Coaches say they have no capacity. Therefore **keep
> coaches out of the experiment**: LE owns recruitment, baseline, weekly tracking and feedback
> collection; coaches continue business-as-usual with no differential support. This **protects
> internal validity and costs ~0 additional coach capacity** — the two objections cancel.

Then: **run in Urban only** — stable connectivity, usable baseline, and the sync fault would corrupt
Rural's weekly tracking anyway; this also protects fragile Rural delivery. **Start date:** not next
Monday. Not because it is unwelcome, but because Monday collides with the AEO action plan, two absent
coaches and the visit — and a baseline collected that week is worthless. A short, specific delay
(1–2 weeks) with a named readiness condition is a strength, not resistance.

**B (Delay) is defensible** if paired with a specific date and condition. **A (Approve as planned) is
weak** — it ignores both the capacity ceiling and the contamination warning. **E alone** is partially
right but leaves the coach-load problem unsolved.

**Excellence markers:** noting that 20 vs 20 is a **small sample** and asking what effect size it can
detect; naming who owns randomisation; observing that "already approved" is a statement about
authority, not feasibility, and that the RM's job is to deliver it in a form that produces a valid
result.

**Anchors.** **5** — picks one, solves both constraints with one move, region choice justified on
data quality, realistic start condition. **3** — picks one with sound reasoning but addresses either
capacity or validity, not both. **1** — approves as planned, refuses to choose, or rejects the
experiment outright on capacity grounds without engaging its strategic approval.

## Part E — Stakeholder Conflict

### Question 8 — Stakeholder management plan (10 points)

Five columns required per stakeholder: **what they need from you · what you need from them · what you
share · what you negotiate · what you escalate**. The reciprocity column ("what you need from them")
is the one weak answers omit, and it is the one that distinguishes management from service.

| Stakeholder | They need | **You need from them** | You share | You negotiate | You escalate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Programme Director** | Stronger adoption; the 25% increase; a clean review | Air cover, a decision on the trade-off, one-in-one-out discipline | Verified weekly numbers with confidence levels; what is paused and why | Scope vs capacity; timeline for a defensible comparison | Anything needing budget/headcount; refusal to trade scope |
| **Rural AEO** | Consistent visits; a written plan in 48h; alternatives where access fails | Route/access support, school opening confirmation, escalation to school heads | Verified visit data, the alternative coverage model, a weekly update | Realistic visit frequency; what counts as coverage when routes close | Systemic access failures; anything needing FDE authority |
| **LE Team** | Fast, valid experiment implementation | Ownership of data collection; a realistic start date; the analysis plan | Capacity reality; region data-quality picture | Scope, region, start date, who collects | Approval conflicts; contested authority over the start date |
| **Coaches** | Fewer simultaneous initiatives; workload relief; to be heard | Honest field data; blockers flagged early; delivery on the protected work | What was cut and why; what is protected; what is coming | Which activities go; how the reduced load is distributed | Wellbeing/attrition risk; anything unsafe |

**The cross-cutting insight worth rewarding:** the four stakeholders are not equally in conflict. The
AEO and the coaches actually **want the same thing** — consistent presence at schools — and both are
constrained by capacity, which is the Programme Director's decision to make. Framing the AEO's
complaint as *evidence for* the capacity case, rather than another demand to absorb, converts a
conflict into leverage. The AEO's report also independently **corroborates the dashboard integrity
problem** and can be cited to the Programme Director.

**On the AEO's 48 hours specifically:** respond inside the deadline; do not lead with rain and device
faults; state verified coverage honestly; give a named alternative coverage model (remote/phone
coaching, cluster meetings at accessible points, temporary reassignment, AEO-assisted access) with
dates and owners; commit to a weekly written update; and **ask for something** — route access and
school opening confirmation. The explanation belongs at the end, as context, not as the answer.

**Anchors.** **5** — all five columns for all four stakeholders, reciprocity real, at least one
alliance or leverage insight, AEO handled without defensiveness. **3** — complete grid, sensible, but
purely service-oriented and treats all four as separate demands. **1** — columns missing, stakeholders
merged, or the AEO answered with excuses.

## Part F — The Hardest Decision

### Question 9 — Top 5 actions in 72 hours (10 points)

Five elements per action: **why first · owner · what you deprioritize · risk reduced · evidence it
worked**. The *deprioritize* clause is the honesty test — 72 hours at 115% capacity means something
must not happen, and naming it is the point.

⚠️ **Note the internal inconsistency:** Part I says a **donor delegation in 10 days**; Part F says
**"the Ministry visit is in five days."** These may be two events or a drafting slip. Treat any
coherent reading charitably. A candidate who **flags the ambiguity and states their assumption** is
demonstrating exactly the discipline the case rewards — do not penalise either reading.

A defensible ranked five:

1. **AEO action plan drafted and sent** (owner: RM with Rural Lead Coach). First because it is the
   only hard external deadline inside the 72 hours, and the relationship carries the access the whole
   Rural operation depends on. Deprioritize: internal reporting polish. Risk reduced: escalation to
   FDE, loss of school access. Evidence: AEO acknowledges and agrees the plan.
2. **Triage the data faults enough to have one defensible number set** (owner: RM + MIS/data focal).
   Not a full fix — a verified subset for the visit and the review. Deprioritize: full dashboard
   remediation, impossible in 72 hours. Risk reduced: briefing leadership or a donor on wrong
   numbers. Evidence: a reconciled figure set with known confidence.
3. **Cover the two absent coaches' critical schools** (owner: Lead Coaches). Deprioritize: the DC
   observation census. Risk reduced: further missed visits feeding the AEO complaint. Evidence: every
   priority school has a named coach for the week.
4. **Reset the LE experiment start with LE** (owner: RM + LE lead). Move off Monday; agree the
   redesigned model from Q7. Deprioritize: the original start date. Risk reduced: an invalid
   experiment and a capacity breach in the worst possible week. Evidence: written agreement on scope,
   region, date and who collects.
5. **Visit preparation, honestly framed** (owner: RM + selected school's coach). Prepare the school
   that shows the **real** story rather than staging one. Deprioritize: the 25% observation increase,
   formally paused in writing with the Programme Director. Risk reduced: a visit that collapses under
   questioning; leadership surprised later. Evidence: a walkthrough completed and a one-page honest
   briefing note agreed with the Programme Director.

Order is arguable — the AEO deadline and the data triage can reasonably swap, and a candidate who
puts coach coverage first on duty-of-care grounds has a real argument. **Reward the reasoning.** What
should *not* happen: promising all of it, or leaving the 25% increase silently unaddressed.

**Anchors.** **5** — five actions, all five elements, explicit deprioritisation including the
observation demand, evidence tests observable within days. **3** — sensible ranked actions but vague
deprioritisation or unmeasurable evidence. **1** — a to-do list, no ranking rationale, nothing given
up, or heroic commitment to everything.

### Question 10 — The five-minute executive response (points not printed — see open issue)

Constraint: **no extra staff, no extra budget, three changes, 90 days, greatest impact on teacher
practice and student learning.** The brief also requires an executive summary of the whole
submission; this is where the two meet.

The test is compression and causality — three changes, each traced to practice **and** to student
outcomes. A strong response opens by briefly and confidently correcting the premise, because the
diagnosis is the reason these three are the right three.

A benchmark set:

1. **Fix the measurement layer first** (offline-first sync + one weekly verified data cut). Not
   glamorous, and the precondition for everything else: every decision in the next 90 days, including
   the expansion decision, is currently made on numbers wrong in a known direction. Frees coach time
   now spent duplicating records. *Practice link:* coaches stop doing clerical work and return to
   classrooms. *Learning link:* resources get aimed at real gaps.
2. **Shift from coverage to dosage** — fewer, deeper coaching cycles on the specific practices FICO
   measures, concentrated where the learning gap is largest. The case's own evidence: 37pp more
   adoption bought 0.3 FICO, while Rural's coaches report practice improves *when they reach teachers
   consistently*. Stop buying breadth that does not convert. *Learning link:* target the
   below-grade-level cohort, where Rural already shows 49% vs Urban's 34% is achievable.
3. **Protect capacity with a standing one-in-one-out rule and a reduced, honest activity set.** 115%
   guarantees everything degrades quietly, and two coaches are already considering leaving. Costs
   nothing; requires only a decision. *Practice link:* coaches with time coach. *Learning link:*
   continuity of coaching is the variable most closely tied to practice change in this case.

**Excellence markers:** stating what will be *stopped* to make room; committing to a measurable
90-day claim ("below-grade-level pass rate in the focus cohort moves from X to Y") rather than
"improved outcomes"; naming the one thing that would make them abandon the plan.

**Anchors.** **5** — three changes, each causally traced to practice and learning, honest about
trade-offs, genuinely deliverable at zero budget, five minutes' worth of content not fifteen. **3** —
three sensible changes, weakly linked to student learning, or quietly requiring resource. **1** — a
wish list, more than three, or changes requiring hiring/budget the constraint forbids.
