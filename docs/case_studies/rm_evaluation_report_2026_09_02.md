## What the pool got right, and what it missed

### Only six of twenty-five read the student data correctly
The key figures cannot be composition shares: Urban's 34% and 75% sum to 109%, Rural's to 110%. Read correctly as subgroup pass rates, **Rural passes 49% of below-grade-level students against Urban's 34%**.

**Twelve candidates read them as composition shares** and proceeded - a factual error against the case data. Most still reached a defensible overall verdict, but by a route that would not survive a donor asking how it was derived. **Hafsa Bashir** went further and concluded Urban performs better across both groups, explicitly rejecting the composition explanation - the reverse of what the data shows.

**Meerab Din, Danish Iqbal and Syed Ateeb Ali** derived the underlying composition (about 20% Urban against 42% Rural below grade level). **Moiz Khan** caught the impossibility and correctly downgraded the comparison to low confidence. **Danish Iqbal** alone tested both readings, rejected one on arithmetic, and confirmed the survivor with a second independent check.

### Only eight priced the demand added after the table
The case asks for a 15-point cut, then separately for 25% more observation coverage - a further 6.25 points on the HITL base, making the real gap closer to 21. **Six submissions produced no closing arithmetic at all.** The strongest not only priced it but funded it from within: **Moiz Khan** allocated all 31 of his freed points, seven to the observation top-up and eight to a time-boxed diagnostic sprint.

### Where the pool was strong
Professional pushback and people leadership were handled well almost across the board - burnout treated as duty of care, reporting failures diagnosed as possible tooling problems before discipline. Several independently found that the contamination warning and the capacity objection share one solution. The best formulation came from **Syed Ateeb Ali**, who solved contamination by blinding coaches to which teachers were in which arm rather than instructing them to withhold support, then proposed checking whether the blind held. That is better than the benchmark's own answer.

## How this was done, and what to distrust

### Disclosure: two anonymity failures, found and fixed
My first verification pass reported the anonymisation clean. It was not. Two names survived and my check missed both: one sat in a Word text box the paragraph walk never reached; one was a surname on a deny-list of tokens that collide with common words, so it was never checked. Both are removed and the corrected files re-uploaded, verified at substring level.

I had already read **Meerab Din's** submission while a surname fragment was visible in a page header. It did not enter the scoring, but you should know it.

A residual limit remains: several candidates identify themselves through the substance of their answers - naming their region, tenure, schools or experiments they have run. Removing that would destroy the evidence that makes those answers strong.

### Method
The benchmark was written and QA'd before any submission was opened, so scoring calibrates to the case rather than to whoever was read first. All 25 were read in full - roughly 203,000 words - and scored from the anonymised files by code, on the case's printed allocation of 100 points across Q1 to Q9. Names were attached only after scoring. Q10 was banded separately and is in no total. Where a candidate reached a different conclusion and defended it with the case data, it scored as high as agreement.

### Calibration - what a second marker should check
A mean of 88 is high. I believe it reflects a genuinely strong internal pool rather than lenient marking, but I cannot rule out drift, and a high mean is exactly the pattern a single unmoderated marker produces. Cheap check: have a second reader independently score the three candidates on the 83 to 86 shelf and the one sitting exactly on 70. If they land within a few points, the ranking holds.
