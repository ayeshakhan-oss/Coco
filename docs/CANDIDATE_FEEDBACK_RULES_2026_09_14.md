# Candidate Feedback: What Changed and Why
**2026-09-14 · everything agreed in one working session · ready to fold into the SOPs**

---

## 1. THE ONE IDEA EVERYTHING ELSE FOLLOWS FROM

> **Explain OUR hiring decision. Do not evaluate THEM as a person.**

We can speak with confidence about **what we needed and could not establish**.
We cannot speak with confidence about **what the candidate lacks, who they are, or what they should do next.**

The emotional arc of every letter:

**"We saw you" → "Here is why we said no" → "We still respect what we saw."**

The candidate should finish thinking: *they genuinely saw me, I understand why they said no, and I still feel respected.*

---

## 2. THE SIX BEHAVIOURS THAT MUST NEVER APPEAR

These are now enforced. Each is reported by name so you can tell them apart.

### 2.1 COACHING — telling them what to do or build
Never say what to develop, learn, gain, document, demonstrate next time, or pursue.

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "If you were to build direct experience inside education systems, whether through a role, through advisory work, or through relationship-building, that could change the picture." | "Direct experience navigating education and government systems carries significant weight for us, because this role needs someone who can enter those environments with contextual fluency from the outset. That was the piece we weren't able to establish." |
| "If you decide to spend time building that understanding and want to reconnect." | *(delete: it means "go get this, then come back")* |

🔴 **A disclaimer does NOT neutralise a prescription.** Adding *"that's your choice to make, not ours to prescribe"* after naming the routes changes nothing. Once the options are named, it has been prescribed.

### 2.2 CAREER DIRECTION — naming their lane
Never tell them which roles, functions or sectors suit them.

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| "You may be better suited to training and facilitation roles." | Tells them where they belong |
| "A Trainer or Coordinator role might fit." | Reads as *you are not senior enough* |
| "If a role opens where corporate systems matter more than government fluency, we'd welcome your application." | Still career direction, just softer |

### 2.3 GRADING AN ANSWER — marking what they said
Reference their stories freely. Never replay a question and assess the answer.

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Your answer about government stakeholders stayed generic." | "We came away wanting a more concrete understanding of how you have navigated government stakeholders and moved work forward within those systems." |
| "When we pushed you, the details thinned out." | *(synthesise, don't narrate the probe)* |
| "Your reasoning didn't deepen." · "You misread the question." | *(delete)* |

### 2.4 PERSON-LEVEL JUDGEMENT — verdicts on who they are
One hiring process does not license a conclusion about a person.

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "Your motivation felt circumstantial rather than mission-driven." | "We weren't able to understand your connection to this particular mission as deeply as we needed to for this role." |
| "You lack strategic thinking." | "We needed stronger evidence of strategic thinking at an organisational level than we were able to establish." |
| "That's not something you can know without having been inside one." | "For this role, we needed that understanding grounded in direct experience, and we weren't able to establish enough of that." |
| "It reveals something important about who you are." · "That's who you are." · "You're coachable." | *(delete: we do not certify people)* |

**Praise counts too.** Describe what stayed with us; do not hand down a verdict.

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "That's rare." · "That's the kind of maturity that will take you far." | "That moment stayed with us because it reflected a willingness to put the outcome ahead of personal ownership." |

### 2.5 REPLAYING EVIDENCE — and the checklist trap
Never narrate a question or scenario we posed and then say what was missing.

🔴 **The worst form, and the easiest to miss, is a list of everything they failed to demonstrate:**

> ❌ "No specific approach to reading the room. No concrete tactic for building leverage. No sense of how you would navigate someone who has power."

That is an improvement checklist for their next interview. It contains no banned words, which is exactly why it slipped through for so long.

> ✅ "For this role, we needed a level of tactical and contextual fluency in navigating government and education systems that we weren't able to establish strongly enough through the process."

**Then stop.** Do not list the missing sub-components afterwards.

For a CV-stage rejection this extends to the written application: never name a question, quote an answer back, or point out that something went unanswered.

> ❌ "The application asked how you handled ambiguity and your response was 'NAAAAA'."
> ✅ "We weren't able to get enough insight into how you've navigated ambiguity, difficult trade-offs and changing priorities."

### 2.6 PRIVATE-NOTE LEAKAGE — the hiring manager's words
**The scorecard is internal shorthand.** Managers write it fast, for colleagues, in blunt language. They are not writing to the candidate. The letter is candidate-facing and may be forwarded, screenshotted or posted publicly.

Use the scorecard as **evidence of what happened**. Write every sentence in our own warm words.

| Scorecard said | Letter must say |
|---|---|
| "Motivation reads circumstantial, wants out of a night-shift job" | "We weren't able to understand your connection to this particular mission as deeply as we needed to." |
| "Stayed generic under the role-play, no concrete tactic" | "We came away wanting a more concrete picture of how you would open a door inside a government system." |
| "Enthusiastic and coachable but unproven on the core skill" | What we could see clearly, versus what we needed and could not yet establish. |
| "Struggled to track the conversation, misread my closing question" | **Nothing at all.** A moment, not a capability. Naming it serves nothing the candidate can use. |

**The test:** if they read this sentence aloud to a friend, or posted it, would it be fair *and* kind?

## 3. THE ENDING

Leave the relationship with dignity. It should say: we saw real strengths, we had a specific reason for saying no to **this** role, and both are true at once.

- No homework. No development advice.
- 🔴 **No conditions on reapplying.** Welcoming them back is good; making it conditional on fixing the gap is homework.

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| "We'd welcome your application again if you take on work that gives you that experience." | "We would be glad to hear from you again, and we hope our paths cross." |

---

## 4. PREFERRED CONSTRUCTIONS

- "For this role, we needed…"
- "We weren't able to establish…"
- "We came away wanting to understand…"
- "What remained unclear to us was…"
- "The distinction mattered for this role because…"
- "Ultimately, this is where our decision landed."

**Avoid:** "You didn't…" · "You failed to…" · "You need to…" · "You should…" · "You aren't ready…" · "Your next step is…"

---

## 5. STRUCTURE AND LENGTH

- **Headings are LOCKED.** Unchanged across all four types.
  - CV rejection: *What we appreciated* / *Where we found questions* / *What we think you should do next*
  - Values feedback: *What We Liked Most About You* / *Where We Found Ourselves Sitting With Questions* / *What We Think You Should Do Next*
  - Warm bench & GWC: *What Stayed With Us* / *Here's the Honest Part* / *Where We Want to Leave This*
- 🔴 **A HEADING IS NOT AN INSTRUCTION.** "What we think you should do next" is a fixed label, not permission to advise. That section is a warm close.
- **800 words minimum**, every feedback letter.
- 🔴 **Never shorten to make it warmer.** If a passage sounds like coaching, use that same space to explain the role, our bar, what we appreciated, and what stayed unclear. Length comes from specificity about **their own material**, never from advice.
- Roughly three rich paragraphs of what genuinely stayed with us; one substantive explanation of the decision.
- **P.S.** = something genuinely nice and specific about them. Not a lecture, not advice, not a summary of the decision.

## 6. WHAT THE WRITER READS (fixed today — it was wrong)

| Letter type | Evidence it now gets |
|---|---|
| CV rejection | The candidate's **actual CV**, cover letter, application answers. *No values scorecard.* |
| Values feedback | Values scorecard |
| **Warm bench** | **Values scorecard AND GWC scorecard**, including the per-question Get It / Want It / Capacity answers |
| GWC rejection | GWC scorecard |

**If the evidence is empty, the app now refuses to draft** rather than writing from nothing.

*Two real failures behind this: 27 CV rejections were sent live written from only a first name and a role title, and warm-bench letters never received the GWC scorecard at all.*

---

## 7. HOW IT IS ENFORCED NOW

**Layer 1 — the writer** is given the tone rules, the per-type SOP, and the candidate's real evidence.

**Layer 2 — a word list** catches ~88 known phrasings, grouped and reported by behaviour so you can tell coaching from grading from judgement.

**Layer 3 — a second reader (NEW).** After the draft is written, another pass reads the whole letter and judges it **by meaning**, then rewrites offending sentences before anyone sees it.

🔴 **Why layer 3 was necessary:** a word list matches spellings, not meaning. Measured on one real draft, the word list reported 3 phrases while the letter contained **11 more violations of the same rules**, none of them matchable — "pushed back" vs "pushed you", "pulled toward" vs "pulling toward", "it's genuinely rare" vs "that's rare" — plus a whole checklist paragraph containing no banned word at all.

**If the second reader cannot run, the draft says so** with a "Semantic review did not run" warning. A letter that skipped the check used to look identical to one that passed it.

---

## 8. THINGS TO WATCH

1. **The closing section drifts first.** Every time a letter slipped, it slipped there. Write it last, read it twice.
2. **Paraphrase is the remaining risk.** The second reader catches meaning, but read the first few letters yourself to confirm it is holding.
3. **The scorecard is the source of the harshest language.** Where a letter turns cold, the sentence usually traces back to a hiring manager's note.
4. **"Case study" is NOT jargon.** The candidate wrote it. GWC, KCD, "warm bench" and "values scorecard" are ours and must never appear.
5. **Quoting the candidate's own words is right and required** in interview-stage letters, and their "I" and "my" inside those quotes is correct.
