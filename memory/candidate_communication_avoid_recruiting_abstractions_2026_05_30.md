# AVOID RECRUITING ABSTRACTIONS — CRITICAL RULE

**Status:** 🔒 LOCKED IN — Apply to ALL candidate communication

**Core Principle:** Describe the PERSON, not the PROFILE.

---

## FORBIDDEN PHRASES (Replace Always)

❌ **Generic candidate labels:**
- "good candidate"
- "strong candidate"
- "impressive profile"
- "strong background"
- "capable person"
- "qualified applicant"
- "excellent fit"
- "high potential"

❌ **Generic capability phrases:**
- "Your profile is strong"
- "It's not about you not being capable"
- "You're well-qualified"
- "You have the right skills"
- "You meet our requirements"

❌ **Generic fit language:**
- "good fit"
- "strong alignment"
- "not a perfect match"
- "fit between profile and role"

---

## WHAT TO DO INSTEAD

### Rule 1: Describe Observed Behaviors, Not Categories

**Instead of:** "Your profile and background are genuinely strong"

**Say:** "The way you identify gaps, take initiative without being asked, and follow through even when it's hard—that showed up consistently"

**Instead of:** "You're a capable person"

**Say:** "What the panel saw was someone who doesn't look away from hard things. You felt the guilt about the design team's work being unusable, AND you stayed until 11pm to rebuild it anyway."

---

### Rule 2: Describe Demonstrated Strengths, Not Résumé Attributes

**Instead of:** "Your grant writing background is strong"

**Say:** "The grant proposals we reviewed showed how you think about stakeholder concerns—you anticipated objections and built them into your language"

**Instead of:** "You have excellent project management skills"

**Say:** "How you coordinated the Abu Dhabi RFP rewrite—pulling in colleagues, staying late, managing the emotional cost—that's systems thinking under pressure"

---

### Rule 3: Describe Organizational Needs, Not Generic Fit

**Instead of:** "You're not a good fit for this role"

**Say:** "We're moving into a specific 18-month phase where we need to deepen our grant-writing capacity and multi-stakeholder coordination infrastructure. What showed up in your background is different—your ability to think systematically, build consensus, see the human cost of decisions. Those are real strengths, but they're not the exact capabilities that will move us forward most directly in this moment."

**Instead of:** "There's misalignment"

**Say:** "The capabilities we need most urgently right now and the capabilities that showed up most prominently in your experience—those are different things."

---

### Rule 4: Explain Through Concrete Business Realities

**Instead of:** "It's a difficult decision between two strong candidates"

**Say:** "We're building X. You bring Y. Another moment might have chosen Y, but this 18-month window specifically requires X. That's not a judgment; it's a business reality."

**Instead of:** "You have excellent qualities but..."

**Say:** "The integrity you demonstrated, the ownership you took under pressure, the way you approached difficult situations—those left a strong impression. AND the specific capabilities we need to build in fundraising right now are grant-writing infrastructure and multi-stakeholder coordination. That's a real difference in what we need versus what you developed most."

---

## THE TEST

After writing your decision explanation, ask:

1. **Am I describing a person or a profile?**
   - ✅ Person: "How you approached the RFP crisis showed me..."
   - ❌ Profile: "Your project management skills are strong"

2. **Am I describing observed behavior or recruiting categories?**
   - ✅ Behavior: "You stopped the meeting and said..."
   - ❌ Category: "You're a capable leader"

3. **Would an ATS, recruiter, or template have written this sentence?**
   - ✅ This feels personal: "The way you carried guilt about the design team's work AND still delivered—that's character"
   - ❌ This sounds generic: "You're a hardworking, dedicated person"

4. **Would this sentence still make sense if we removed all hiring terminology?**
   - ✅ Yes: "You stopped the meeting and said either invite this person or don't discuss them"
   - ❌ No: "You're a strong fit for high-potential candidates"

---

## GOAL: THE EMOTIONAL OUTCOME

**Candidate should feel:**
✅ "They understood who I am"  
✅ "They saw me as a person with character, not a resume"  
✅ "The decision makes sense because they explained their actual need"  
✅ "I can hold my head up—the issue was timing/strategy, not my capability"

**NOT:**
❌ "They assessed my profile"  
❌ "I was scored and ranked"  
❌ "I don't know why I wasn't chosen"  
❌ "They think I'm not good enough"

---

## EXAMPLES: BEFORE & AFTER

### Example 1: Decision Rationale

**BEFORE (Recruiting abstraction):**
"Your background is strong, but you're not the right fit for this specific moment."

**AFTER (Observed behavior + concrete need):**
"What showed up in your background—your ability to think systematically, to build consensus across teams, to see the human cost of decisions—those are real strengths. But they're not the exact capabilities that will move us forward most directly in this 18-month phase. We specifically need to deepen our grant-writing capacity and multi-stakeholder coordination infrastructure."

---

### Example 2: Compliment

**BEFORE (Generic label):**
"You're a strong candidate with excellent leadership skills."

**AFTER (Observed behavior):**
"You stopped a management meeting mid-flow and said either invite the person being discussed or don't have this conversation. The team changed its approach because of you. That's not a skill you learned. That's someone whose integrity is not negotiable."

---

### Example 3: Feedback

**BEFORE (Recruiting abstraction):**
"You need to improve your communication skills to be more engaging."

**AFTER (Concrete observation):**
"How energetic and proactive you come across in real time—that resonated differently with different people in the room. Some felt you were enthusiastically present. Others sensed intensity that felt like you might interrupt. Not a flaw. Just a note about how you present yourself in the moment."
