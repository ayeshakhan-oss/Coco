# CANDIDATE COMMUNICATION QUALITY REVIEW PROTOCOL

**Status:** 🔒 LOCKED IN — Use for ALL candidate rejection, warm bench, GWC, and feedback emails

**When to Use:** Before finalizing ANY candidate email. Non-negotiable.

---

## THE 10-POINT CHECKLIST

### 1. Balance of Evidence
- [ ] **Identify** every piece of praise in the email
- [ ] **Identify** the actual hiring decision
- [ ] **Check:** Is the evidence supporting praise equally specific as the explanation supporting the decision?
- [ ] **If not:** Strengthen decision explanation until both match specificity level
- **Why:** Candidate shouldn't think "if you valued all this, why wasn't I selected?"

### 2. Avoid Generic Candidate Labels
❌ **NEVER USE:**
- "good candidate"
- "strong candidate"
- "impressive candidate"
- "high-potential candidate"
- "excellent profile"
- "top-tier talent"

✅ **INSTEAD, DESCRIBE ACTUAL OBSERVED CHARACTER:**
- Their judgment
- Their integrity
- Their courage
- Their learning mindset
- Their leadership
- Their self-awareness
- Their way of treating people
- Their decision-making under pressure

**Example:**
- ❌ "You're a genuinely good candidate"
- ✅ "The panel saw someone with unusual integrity"

### 3. Developmental Feedback Must Be Concrete
- [ ] Is any feedback vague or unclear?
- [ ] **If yes:** Either make it more concrete OR remove it entirely
- [ ] Never leave a candidate with abstract criticism

**Example:**
- ❌ "Your communication could be more energetic"
- ✅ "How energetic and proactive you come across in real time resonated differently with different people in the room"

### 4. Endorsement Level Test
- [ ] Could the candidate reasonably conclude: "If they believed all this, why wasn't I selected?"
- [ ] **If yes:** Your praise-to-decision ratio is imbalanced
- [ ] Rebalance: Either increase decision specificity OR decrease praise generalization

### 5. Evidence Support
- [ ] Is every major compliment earned through evidence, examples, quotes, behaviors, or stories?
- [ ] Remove any generic praise not supported by specific observations
- [ ] If you can't cite an example, the compliment shouldn't be there

### 6. Emotional Arc (4 Elements)
- [ ] **Candidate understands the decision** (clear explanation of why they weren't selected)
- [ ] **Candidate understands why** (concrete rationale tied to their profile vs. other candidate)
- [ ] **Candidate understands what was valued** (specific character observations, not labels)
- [ ] **Candidate leaves with dignity** (warm tone, future door open, they are seen as a person)
- [ ] **Check:** Does the email flow naturally through these 4 elements?

### 7. Character Over Assessment
**Replace evaluative labels with character observations:**

| ❌ Label | ✅ Character |
|----------|--------------|
| "good candidate" | "the consistency between what you believe and how you behave when those beliefs are tested" |
| "strong candidate" | "your self-awareness and clarity about what you know and what you're still learning" |
| "impressive" | "the way you carry responsibility even when the outcome is uncertain" |
| "high-potential" | "your willingness to learn things that scare you" |

**Goal:** Candidate feels SEEN, not SCORED.

### 8. Haroon Yasin Balance Rule (CRITICAL)
- [ ] **Count:** How many specific praise examples are in the email?
- [ ] **Count:** How many specific decision rationale examples are in the email?
- [ ] **These counts should be equal or nearly equal**
- [ ] If praise examples = 3 stories, decision explanation should have ~3 concrete details
- [ ] **Example:** If you mention 3 specific interview moments praising the candidate, you must give 1-3 equally specific reasons for the decision

**Why:** Prevents the "if you valued all this" paradox.

### 9. The Rejection Rationale Must Be Concrete
- [ ] Is the decision rationale as specific as the praise?
- [ ] ❌ "We made a choice that reflected something specific about this moment"
- [ ] ✅ "We had another candidate whose background in X aligned more directly with our needs in Y"

**Pattern:**
- Vague: situational, narrow, timing, specific moment needed
- Concrete: other candidate's experience, our strategic need, specific skill gap, timeline requirement

### 10. The Final Test
**Ask yourself:** "If I were the candidate receiving this email, would the admiration feel genuine AND would the rejection still make complete sense?"

- [ ] If YES → Send
- [ ] If NO → Revise until both are true

---

## FINAL CHECK: EMOTIONAL HONESTY

**The candidate should leave feeling:**
- ✅ "We saw who I am" (character, not categories)
- ✅ "We genuinely valued what we saw" (specific evidence)
- ✅ "The decision makes sense" (concrete rationale)
- ✅ "I have dignity" (warm tone, door open)

**NOT:**
- ❌ "We evaluated my profile"
- ❌ "Generic compliments they could give anyone"
- ❌ "I still don't understand why I wasn't selected"
- ❌ "They're just being nice to soften the blow"

---

## IMPLEMENTATION CHECKLIST

Before sending ANY candidate email:

1. ✅ Read through looking for generic labels → replace with character observations
2. ✅ Count specific praise examples → count specific decision examples → balance them
3. ✅ Check decision rationale concreteness → must match praise concreteness
4. ✅ Remove vague feedback → make concrete OR delete
5. ✅ Verify endorsement doesn't exceed decision (the "why wasn't I hired?" test)
6. ✅ Trace emotional arc: decision → why → what we valued → dignity
7. ✅ Read aloud → does it sound genuine or formulaic?
8. ✅ Final test: "Would I feel seen or scored if I received this?"

## REFERENCE: BALANCED EMAIL STRUCTURE

**PRAISE SECTION (Specific):**
```
You described a moment where you stopped a management meeting mid-flow. 
You told colleagues "either invite this person so they hear feedback directly, 
or do not discuss them here." The team changed its approach because of you.
```

**DECISION SECTION (Must be equally specific):**
```
We had another candidate whose background in fundraising operations and 
partnership strategy aligned more directly with where we need to move. 
Their experience in grant writing and multi-stakeholder coordination was 
a tighter fit for this specific 18-month roadmap.
```

Notice: Both sections have 1-2 concrete details. They match in specificity level.
