# Evidence-Based Rejection Rationale

**Status: LOCKED PRINCIPLE (First Implementation: Mizhgan Kirmani GWC rejection, 2026-06-01)**

## Core Principle

When explaining why a candidate was not selected, the evidence supporting the decision must be **as specific and concrete as the evidence supporting praise**. The Haroon Yasin balance rule prevents the asymmetry that makes rejections feel unfair: candidates seeing themselves praised for one thing but rejected for vague reasons.

## The Balance Rule

**For every specific praise example in "What Stayed With Us," there must be a correspondingly specific decision example in "Here's the Honest Part."**

Examples of BALANCE:
- Praise: "In the case study, when you encountered a technical blocker, you immediately sketched three approaches on the whiteboard and explained trade-offs" (specific, observable)
- Decision: "For this role's requirements around rapid prototyping under uncertainty, we need comfort diving into code immediately. Your approach — sketch-then-code — is thoughtful but takes longer than this role allows" (equally specific, concrete)

Examples of IMBALANCE (❌):
- Praise: "You showed strong analytical thinking throughout the interview" (generic)
- Decision: "Unfortunately we felt the fit wasn't quite right" (vague)

## The "Can You Show Me?" Test

For every point in "Here's the Honest Part," ask: **"Can the candidate show me exactly what I did that led to this conclusion?"**

If the answer is no — if you can't point to a specific quote, moment, or observable behavior — the rejection rationale is too abstract. Rewrite it with concrete details.

Examples:

| Too Abstract | Concrete |
|---|---|
| "You lack executive presence" | "In the stakeholder interview, you took detailed notes but didn't articulate a viewpoint on the business tradeoffs being discussed. We need someone who shapes the room, not just absorbs it." |
| "Your leadership style is collaborative" (as gap) | "In the breakout session, you asked clarifying questions but didn't propose a direction. This role needs someone comfortable saying 'Here's what I think we should do' even when the group is uncertain." |
| "You weren't energized by the mission" | "In your final comments, you talked about personal career growth and learning. We didn't hear connection to Taleemabad's specific mission of equitable education. That's not a knock — it means this role isn't your north star." |

## Connection to "No Intent Inference"

This rule works hand-in-hand with [[lesson_no_intent_inference_rejection_emails_2026_06_01]].

**Related principle (no intent-words):** Never tell candidates *what they thought or believed*. Stay with what they *did or said*.

**Evidence-based rule (this file):** When you name what they did, be as detailed as possible.

Example:
- ❌ "You weren't committed to this challenge" (assumes mental state)
- ❌ "You seemed hesitant about the technical scope" (assumes thought)
- ✅ "You said 'I'd want to learn more before committing' when asked about the migration timeline. We need someone who commits first, learns during. That's a difference in approach, not commitment." (observable behavior + concrete context)

## Implementation in Email

**"What Stayed With Us" section:**
- 2-3 specific moments with exact timestamps or quotes
- Show *what they did and said* that impressed the panel
- Make it detailed enough that they recognize themselves in it

**"Here's the Honest Part" section:**
- 2-3 specific gaps, equally detailed
- Frame each as: "When [observable situation], you [observable action/statement]. For this role, we need [concrete alternative]."
- Never abstract to personal qualities

## The Role-Specific Frame

Always contextualize the gap as **role-specific, not personal**.

Example:
- ❌ "You don't think like a founder" (character judgment)
- ✅ "This role requires constant pivoting based on real-time revenue signals. Your strength is deep implementation — once direction is set, you execute beautifully. Those are different rhythms." (difference in fit, not value)

## Why This Matters

1. **Fairness:** Candidates can't improve if they don't know what to improve. Vague rejections feel random.
2. **Dignity:** Specific, grounded feedback feels respectful because it's not dismissive. It says "we see you clearly."
3. **Career Impact:** A candidate who knows "I'm excellent at X but this role needs Y" can find the right role. A candidate who hears "it wasn't a fit" is left guessing.

## Pre-Send Checklist

Before sending any warm bench or GWC rejection email:

- [ ] Count the specific moments/paragraphs in "What Stayed With Us" (aim for 2-3)
- [ ] Count the specific moments/paragraphs in "Here's the Honest Part" (should be ≈ same count)
- [ ] For each praise example, can you answer: "What exactly did they do or say?"
- [ ] For each gap example, can you answer: "What exactly did they do or say that showed this gap?"
- [ ] Is the gap framed as role-specific, not a personal failing?
- [ ] Would the candidate recognize themselves in both sections?
