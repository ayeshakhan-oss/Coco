# GWC REJECTION EMAILS — BEFORE & AFTER (2026-05-30)

**Summary:** GWC rejection emails now follow warm bench tone, headings, word count, and character-focused language standards.

---

## BEFORE (OLD STANDARD)

### Word Count
- **400-770 words acceptable**
- OK to go lower if scorecard data limited

### Opening
```
Dear [Candidate],

We have completed our review of your technical assessment. 
We want to let you know that we will not be moving you forward at this time.
```

### Headings (5 sections)
1. "What We Liked Most About You"
2. "Where We Found Ourselves Sitting With Questions"
3. "The Gap"
4. "What Matters Next"
5. "Closing"

### Tone & Language
- Warm, mentoring
- "We" voice
- Evidence-based
- Honest but encouraging
- Generic: "You demonstrated the ability to think..."
- No GWC jargon

### Example Language (OLD)
```
"What We Liked Most About You"
You demonstrated the ability to think across the full scope of a technical project.
```

---

## AFTER (WARM BENCH ALIGNED — 2026-05-30)

### Word Count
- **800+ words MANDATORY**
- Only lower if truly insufficient scorecard content
- Aim for 800+ at any cost (expand with deeper analysis)

### Opening (EXACT)
```
This is not a yes for now.

But we need to tell you something about what we saw in your interview 
that the panel kept discussing afterward, because it reveals 
something important about who you are and how you approach your work.
```

### Headings (4 sections + P.S.)
1. "What Stayed With Us"
2. "Here's the Honest Part"
3. "Where We Want to Leave This"
4. P.S.

### Tone & Language
- Warm, observational (not clinical)
- "We" voice
- Evidence-based (scorecard + interview moments)
- Character-focused (integrity, courage, learning mindset)
- NO recruiting abstractions ("good candidate" → observed behaviors)
- No GWC jargon
- Show company perspective/vulnerability
- Specific interview moments with timestamps

### Example Language (NEW)
```
"What Stayed With Us"
When you described your cricket analysis system, you articulated a 
multi-layered approach that showed systems-level thinking. You discussed 
multiple data sources: Kaggle, ESPN, Crickinfo. More importantly, 
you showed awareness that different data sources require different 
integration strategies. That's not just listing tools. You were thinking 
about trade-offs and constraints.
```

## KEY CHANGES

### 1. Opening Line (CRITICAL)
**BEFORE:** Generic "We will not move forward"
**AFTER:** "This is not a yes for now" + specific interview moment

**Why:** Warm bench opening is more human, specific, acknowledges company thinking

### 2. Heading Structure (LOCKED FROM WARM BENCH)
**BEFORE:** 5 different headings
**AFTER:** 3 warm bench headings + P.S.

**Why:** Consistency across rejection types (values, warm bench, GWC all use same headings)

### 3. Word Count (MANDATORY)
**BEFORE:** 400-770 (flexible)
**AFTER:** 800+ (firm, only lower if truly insufficient content)

**Why:** Allows deeper analysis, more specific evidence, shows genuine engagement with feedback

### 4. Language (CHARACTER-FOCUSED)
**BEFORE:** "You demonstrated the ability..."
**AFTER:** "When you described [specific moment], you showed [observed behavior]. Here's why that matters..."

**Why:** Candidate feels SEEN (character), not SCORED (profile)

### 5. Scorecard Integration
**BEFORE:** Extract scores, write generic feedback
**AFTER:** Extract scores + quote interview evidence, weave together, explain WHY

**Why:** More human, specific, grounded in actual conversation

### 6. P.S. (NOW REQUIRED)
**BEFORE:** Optional
**AFTER:** REQUIRED - Ties back to powerful moment

**Why:** Memorable closing, candidate should screenshot it

## WHAT STAYS THE SAME

✅ Use GWC scorecard data ONLY (no fabrication)
✅ 2-3 scorecard strengths (What Stayed With Us)
✅ 2-3 scorecard gaps (Here's the Honest Part)
✅ Actionable guidance (Where We Want to Leave This)
✅ Warm, mentoring tone
✅ "We" voice
✅ No em dashes
✅ No jargon (GWC, Zero In Call, names)
✅ Taleemabad email format (locked)

## EXAMPLE: BEFORE vs. AFTER

### BEFORE Opening
```
Dear Ali,

We have completed our review of your technical assessment for the Hackathon 2026 position. 
We want to let you know that we will not be moving you forward at this time. 
We also want to share what we observed during our conversation with you, 
because the feedback is grounded in what you actually showed us, 
and deserves to be specific rather than generic.
```

### AFTER Opening
```
This is not a yes for now.

But we need to tell you something about what we saw in your interview 
that the panel kept discussing afterward, because it reveals something 
important about who you are and how you approach your work.

When you described your cricket analysis and prediction system, 
you articulated a multi-layered approach that showed systems-level thinking. 
You discussed multiple data sources: Kaggle, ESPN, Crickinfo. 
More importantly, you showed awareness that different data sources require 
different integration strategies. That kind of thinking—recognizing that 
the architecture must adapt to the data—is valuable.
```

## BEFORE: What We Liked Most
```
You demonstrated the ability to think across the full scope of a technical project 
from data sourcing through implementation to user interface. When you described your 
cricket analysis and prediction system, you articulated a multi-layered approach 
that showed systems-level thinking.
```

## AFTER: What Stayed With Us
```
When you described your cricket analysis and prediction system, you articulated a 
multi-layered approach that showed systems-level thinking. You discussed multiple 
data sources: Kaggle datasets, live data from ESPN and Crickinfo through web scraping. 
More importantly, you showed awareness that different data sources require different 
integration strategies. That kind of thinking—recognizing that the architecture must 
adapt to the data—is valuable.

You also demonstrated genuine knowledge of ML model selection. You could discuss when 
to use Random Forest versus XGBoost for different prediction scenarios, and you 
understood that real-time data ingestion creates fundamentally different technical 
requirements than batch processing. You weren't just listing tools. You were thinking 
about trade-offs and constraints. That's systems-level thinking, and it's rare.
```

**Difference:**
- More specific (names sources, models)
- Deeper analysis (WHY it matters)
- Character-focused (thinking patterns, approach)
- More evidence (quotes, details)
