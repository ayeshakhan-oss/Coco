# No Intent Inference in Rejection Emails

**CRITICAL PRINCIPLE (2026-06-01):** Never tell candidates what they assumed, believed, thought, preferred, or were energized by.

---

## The Problem

Intent inferences sound like mind-reading.

They make rejection emails feel prosecutorial rather than mentoring.

Candidates stop trusting the feedback.

**Example - What NOT to do:**

> "You appeared to assume that preparation wasn't necessary because your ability to think on your feet would be sufficient."

This tells the candidate what she was thinking. She can't defend herself against mind-reading.

---

## The Solution

Stay with observations and unanswered questions.

Never infer mental states.

**Example - What TO do:**

> "What the panel left wanting was clearer evidence of how you think when given time to prepare, sit with a problem, form your own view, and test assumptions independently before entering the room."

This describes the observation (didn't have fully formed view beforehand) and the gap (we wanted to see that thinking). No mind-reading.

---

## Forbidden Words in Rejection Emails

❌ "You assumed"  
❌ "You believed"  
❌ "You thought"  
❌ "You preferred"  
❌ "You were energized by"  
❌ "You weren't appreciating"  
❌ "You were thinking that..."  
❌ "You didn't understand that..."  

---

## How to Replace Intent Inferences

### Pattern 1: Incomplete preparation

**❌ WRONG:**
> "You appeared to assume that preparation wasn't necessary because communication skills would carry through."

**✅ RIGHT:**
> "What the panel left wanting was clearer evidence of how you think when given time to prepare, test assumptions, and develop your own perspective independently."

**Why:** Describes the gap without telling her what she was thinking.

---

### Pattern 2: Mental state assumption

**❌ WRONG:**
> "You weren't yet appreciating how different Taleemabad's challenge is."

**✅ RIGHT:**
> "What left the panel uncertain was whether you had fully grasped the magnitude of that difference."

**Why:** Asks the question rather than asserting a conclusion about her mental state.

---

### Pattern 3: Speculating about motivation

**❌ WRONG:**
> "You're more energized by stewardship in established environments, not creation in uncertain ones."

**✅ RIGHT:**
> "Your experience has been primarily shaped by stewardship of existing donor relationships. What we wanted to see evidence of was donor creation: identifying cold relationships, building credibility where it doesn't exist, creating momentum for organizations still earning their place."

**Why:** Describes what the panel observed and wanted, not what drives her.

---

### Pattern 4: Dismissing capability via mental judgment

**❌ WRONG:**
> "Your mindset is order-first, not chaos-first."

**✅ RIGHT:**
> "Your strength is bringing order to complexity. This role requires comfort building in the chaos first, with order emerging later. Those are different muscles."

**Why:** Acknowledges her strength while describing the different requirement. No psychological label.

---

## The Core Rule

**Observation:** "The interview didn't address X."  
**Question:** "What we left uncertain was whether..."  
**NOT:** "You clearly believe Y" or "You prefer Z"

## Examples from Mizhgan Kirmani Email (2026-06-01)

**Corrected - No Intent Inference:**

### Before (WRONG):
> "She relied on communication skills that are genuinely strong to move forward without the foundational work done first. But the interview left us with a specific concern: you appeared to assume that preparation wasn't necessary because your ability to think on your feet would be sufficient."

### After (RIGHT):
> "The case study became important in the discussion because it was one of the few opportunities we had to see how you engage with a problem before a conversation begins. You hadn't arrived with a fully formed perspective. Instead, you worked through many of the questions in real time with us. Your communication skills carried the moment forward productively. What the panel left wanting was clearer evidence of how you think when given time to prepare, sit with a problem, form your own view, and test assumptions independently before entering the room."

**Difference:** No longer saying "you assumed." Just observing the behavior (worked through live) and identifying the gap (wanted to see thinking beforehand).

---

### Before (WRONG):
> "What left us uncertain was whether you had fully internalized how different that challenge is. Not your capability to learn it. But whether you went into the interview understanding that the challenge here is fundamentally different from the one you've mastered at TCF. That question, combined with approaching the case study without prior preparation, left us wanting stronger evidence that you had internalized just how different this particular version of market entry would be."

### After (RIGHT):
> "So the question the interview left us with was not whether you understand fundraising. You clearly do. It was whether this particular kind of fundraising, the kind that begins before donor confidence has been established, is the challenge you were most ready to step into right now."

**Difference:** Simpler, clearer, no speculating about what she internalized. Just asking the question.

---

## Why This Matters

When rejection emails use intent inferences, candidates feel accused.

When they use observations + questions, candidates feel understood.

The same concern lands completely differently.

---

## Application to All Rejection Types

This principle applies to:
- ✅ GWC rejections
- ✅ Warm bench rejections
- ✅ Values interview rejections
- ✅ CV screening rejections
- ✅ Any feedback email

Before sending ANY rejection, scan for intent words. Replace every instance.

---

## Self-QA Rule

**Before sending, ask:** "Does this rejection tell the candidate what they were thinking or believing?"

**If yes:** Rewrite using observations + questions instead.

**Zero tolerance:** No intent inferences in final version.
