# SENT LETTERS - Warm Bench

## Muneeb - 1014 words

**Subject:** When Stepping Back Reveals Everything

### OPENING

Dear NAME, This is not a yes for now. But we need to tell you something about what we
saw in your interview that the panel kept discussing afterward, because it stayed with
us. There's a moment from your values interview that has stayed with us since the panel
ended. You told us about a premier client at your previous job, someone who was on the
verge of handing their project to someone from their personal circle. You could have
accepted the loss. Instead, you went back. You talked it out. You put in the extra
effort. You said to them: 'I had put in my blood and sweat in this project, I assure you
that I will work for you.' You got them on board again. That's not luck.

### What Stayed With Us

was not just that you fought for the relationship, it was the honesty underneath it. You
acknowledged what losing them would have cost you. You didn't pretend it didn't matter.
You owned it, and you showed up anyway. What Stayed With Us There was another moment
that the room kept returning to. You described a rift between two managers at your
previous organisation, both strong performers, but their miscommunication was dragging
down company performance. Nobody owned the problem. You did. You brought them together
and stayed with the conversation until they worked it out. Your instinct was clear: you
had invested in helping them grow and scale. You weren't going to step back just because
it was uncomfortable. That reflex, to lean in rather than step back, was something the
panel came back to more than once. It stayed with us because the problem was not yours
to fix and there was no credit attached to fixing it. What also stayed with us was how
you show up for people who can't speak up. You described your team being asked to work
14-hour days under crunch deadlines, and instead of assuming they'd say something if it
mattered, you took it to the managers yourself. You explained the frustration it was
creating. You said 'it was heard.' That's not a small thing. You didn't wait for
permission to speak. You didn't need credit for it. You just saw something that wasn't
okay and you named it. What stayed with us was that you carried something on behalf of
people who were not in the room to carry it themselves. And through the entire
conversation, there was something else we noticed: you could receive hard feedback and
actually change because of it. You were told you were overworking and needed to be more
present in your personal life. Your first instinct was to resist, you were still
involved in your work. But you sat with it. You took time. You changed gradually. And
now you go for padel and swimming daily. You didn't defend yourself. You listened, you
reflected, you moved.

### Here's the Honest Part

Here's the honest part. During our technical interview, we explored how you might build
access and influence within a bureaucratic environment, specifically within education
and government systems. You demonstrated smart thinking about how systems work and
thoughtful general principles. What we needed and could not establish was concrete,
grounded experience navigating inside those institutions, the contextual fluency that
comes from having worked directly within education policy channels and with the
officials who shape them. Your background with infrastructure approvals gave us a
starting point, but we came away wanting to understand how you'd specifically operate in
education governance environments. That distinction matters for this role because this
work requires someone who can enter those systems with fluency from the outset, not
primarily relying on transferred principles, but grounded in direct experience of how
education and government institutions actually operate. Your enthusiasm for the mission
came through clearly. Your willingness to relocate came through clearly. What we weren't
able to confirm was the foundation of direct experience inside education and government
systems that this particular role, at this particular moment, needs. This is a statement
about what we needed to see for this role, not about what you are able to do. The
requirement here is an unusually specific one, and our decision turned on it rather than
on the range of what you brought us. It is worth being clear about why we weigh this so
heavily, because it is not a general preference for public sector backgrounds. The work
in this role begins inside systems that are already running, with their own timelines,
their own approval routes and their own history of what has been tried before. Someone
arriving without that grounding spends their first year learning what the room already
knows, and the timelines we are working to do not have that year in them. That is the
bar this particular role is held to, and it is where our decision landed.

### Where We Want to Leave This

Where we want to leave this is simple: we saw real strengths in you across your values
interview. We saw someone who doesn't walk away from hard things, who speaks up for
people who can't, who genuinely listens and changes because of it. That matters. We also
had a specific reason for saying no to this particular role, and both are true at once.
Direct experience navigating education and government systems carries significant weight
for us, because this work requires someone who can enter those environments with
contextual fluency from the outset. That was the piece we weren't able to establish
through our conversations. We would be glad to hear from you again. If something opens
here that sits differently against your experience, we would welcome a fresh application
from you. We hope our paths cross again.

### P.S.

That moment when you described your venture's marketing as your 'child idea', and then
decided to hand it to someone who could manage it better because the outcomes mattered
more than ownership, that stayed with us. You named the attachment and the decision in
the same breath, and you chose the outcome over being the one who owned it.

## Salman - 854 words

**Subject:** The Relational Work That Moves Decisions Forward

### OPENING

Dear NAME, This is not a yes for now. We need to tell you something about what we saw
across both interviews, because it stayed with us and shaped where we landed. There is a
moment from your values interview that the panel kept discussing afterward. You were
asked about a time you had courageous conversations, and you told us about the moment
you pushed back on your director's request for a full implementation plan by tomorrow.
You knew it needed primary research, not just secondary sources. You made the case for
time and rigour. You didn't just comply.

### What Stayed With Us

was not just that you advocated for quality, it was that you did it upward, to someone
with authority over you, and you did it because the work mattered more than the
convenience of saying yes. That takes real courage. The panel came back to that moment
more than once. What Stayed With Us There were other moments that shaped what we saw.
You spoke about carrying something deeply difficult without stepping away from it. You
carried a national incubation project for four years through repeated hurdles, shifting
ministries, changing sites, student availability collapsing and rebuilding, all the way
to completion. You fought your own organisation so a colleague's family got what they
were owed, when nobody else would. You advocated your team members' promotions ahead of
your own. You released your own Punjab Startup Portal feasibility work, substantial
effort including benchmarking research, slides, graphs, when leadership judged it was
not a current priority, and you let it go without bitterness. What stayed with us across
these moments was not just that you didn't walk away from hard things. It was that you
walked toward hard things on behalf of other people, without needing the credit or the
win. Your willingness to learn and to let go of what wasn't serving the mission also
stood out. You named AI prompting and storytelling as the two things that recently
changed how you approach work. You completed a rigorous six-week bootcamp on
organisational development and governance that included a delegation to Turkey. You
built a weekly learning relationship with a colleague to close gaps. You fought for and
secured a first-ever training budget where none had existed. You recognised a pattern in
yourself that was not serving the work and shifted how you respond to overload. The
panel noticed that you didn't defend yourself against that insight, you owned it, sat
with it, and moved. That willingness to reflect and change because of what you learn is
something we value deeply.

### Here's the Honest Part

Here's the honest part. Across your technical interview, we explored scenarios where you
would need to build and move decisions forward with government officials, ministers,
secretaries, senior bureaucrats in education ministries. We observed a consistent
pattern: you brought genuine insight into how government institutions work and
demonstrated sound judgment on ethics. Your experience inside PITB gave you real
understanding of stakeholder incentives and decision-making structures. What we needed
to see and could not clearly establish was evidence that you had successfully moved a
pilot arrangement or a key commitment from no to yes through the relational work that
typically precedes those moments. Across multiple scenarios, we noticed you focusing on
problem analysis and the architecture of a solution, rather than on the personal
relationships and trust-building that usually have to happen first with senior officials
in government settings. For this particular role, at this particular moment, we needed
stronger evidence that you had moved a decision forward by building and navigating those
personal channels with people in positions of power. That distinction matters because
this work begins inside systems that are already running, with their own timelines and
approval routes. Someone needs to enter those relationships with fluency and a track
record of having moved decisions inside them before. We were not able to establish that
from the process.

### Where We Want to Leave This

Where we want to leave this is clear: we saw real strengths across both interviews. We
saw someone who leans in rather than retreats, who speaks up when it matters, who
advocates for others ahead of themselves, who listens and changes, and who brings
genuine insight into how systems work. We also had a specific reason for saying no to
this particular role, and both are true at once. The role requires someone who has
successfully built and navigated personal relationships with senior government officials
and moved decisions forward through those channels. That was the piece we were not able
to establish through our conversations. We believe your strengths would translate well
in environments where problem-solving and systems thinking lead. For this particular
mission and stakeholder group, we needed a different foundation to move forward.

### P.S.

That moment when you described releasing your Punjab Startup Portal work, substantial
effort, benchmarking research, all of it, because leadership had moved on, and you let
it go. You named over-explaining as the hardest lesson you learned, and you dropped it.
That willingness to hold your work lightly so the mission could move forward stayed with
us.
