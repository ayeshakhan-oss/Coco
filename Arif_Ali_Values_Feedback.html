Don't Hold On Too Tight
Deep-Dive Evidence
At Microsoft, junior female colleague had superior testing methodology. Rather than defend his approach, he learned from her and adopted her process. No defensive ego; genuine intellectual humility. Currently creates roadmaps but hands projects to junior PMs for execution and development. Doesn't maintain control; explicitly lets go so juniors can develop craft and gain experience. Shows comfort with distributed ownership—enabling others to grow rather than becoming a bottleneck.
Curve-Ball Evidence
Examples demonstrate *process flexibility* and delegation but lack *strategic flexibility*—ability to pivot cherished strategy when market conditions change. Examples are about adopting better techniques (colleague's testing method) and delegating execution (junior PM's roadmap), not about holding strategic direction strongly then admitting it was wrong. For Group PM, "Don't Hold On Too Tight" also means: admitting product hypothesis was wrong, pivoting entire roadmap based on market feedback, changing mind about hire or team structure. Delegation and technical flexibility are solid, but *strategic* letting-go isn't demonstrated.
